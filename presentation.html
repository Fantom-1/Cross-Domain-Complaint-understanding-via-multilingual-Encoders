The Contrastive Solution

Contrastive representation learning maps complaints onto a unit hypersphere, enforcing two fundamental properties:

  • 1. Alignment: Semantically equivalent grievances map close together regardless of phrasing.
  • 2. Uniformity: The representations preserve maximal information by spreading uniformly across all dimensions.
  • 3. Metric Separation: Cross-domain grievances (e.g. debt vs mortgage) are strictly separated by an angular margin.

Methodology 1

Unsupervised SimCSE (Simple Contrastive Learning)

How SimCSE Works Without Any Labels

SimCSE (Gao et al., EMNLP 2021) leverages the stochasticity of standard dropout ($p=0.1$) as a data augmentation technique.

  • Passes the exact same sentence through the encoder twice.
  • Because different neurons are dropped out, two slightly distinct vectors $(\mathbf{z}_i, \mathbf{z}_i^+)$ are produced.
  • Treats $(\mathbf{z}_i, \mathbf{z}_i^+)$ as a positive pair.
  • Treats all other complaints in the mini-batch as negative pairs.
Loss = -log [ exp(sim(z_i, z_i+) / τ) / Σ exp(sim(z_i, z_j) / τ) ]

Methodology 2

Supervised Contrastive Learning (SupCon)

How SupCon Maximizes Class Separation

SupCon (Khosla et al., NeurIPS 2020) integrates grounded category supervisory signals directly into the metric loss function.

  • Pulls together multiple distinct complaints that share the same underlying grievance intent $(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_p)$.
  • Pushes away hard negatives $(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_n)$ from distinct product domains.
  • Employs margin triplet optimization with distance metric penalties:
Loss = max(0, ||z_i - z_p||² - ||z_i - z_n||² + α)
