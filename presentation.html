The Challenge

Why Out-of-the-Box AI Failed at First

1. The "Vector Collapse" Problem

When raw foundation models like XLM-V read financial complaints without fine-tuning, they suffer from representation collapse.

In simple terms: the AI thought almost every complaint sounded 96% identical to every other complaint, regardless of whether it was about medical debt, an overdraft fee, or a mortgage.

Pretrained AI Accuracy (Precision@10):14.11%
Basic Keyword Search (TF-IDF):33.54%

The 778M parameter neural network initially lost to basic keyword matching.

2. The Contrastive Solution

To fix this, we trained the AI using contrastive learning:

Pull Together (Alignment): Complaints describing the exact same consumer problem are pulled tightly together in the AI's mind, even if phrased differently.
Push Apart (Uniformity): Complaints about different topics are forcibly pushed apart across the entire vector space.
The Model

XLM-V: Why 778.5 Million Parameters?

Massive Vocabulary
901,629

Standard models understand 30,000 words. Meta gave XLM-V a huge 901,629-word dictionary to understand complaints in 140+ languages.

Word Dictionary Table
692.8 Million

Just storing definitions for all 901k words takes 692.8M parameters (89% of the entire model). This is why it behaves like a 1-billion model.

Reasoning Layers
85.1 Million

12 deep transformer neural layers that analyze grammatical syntax, dates, financial amounts, and consumer intent.

Projection Head (In Plain English): Takes the AI's 768-dimensional thought vector, refines it through two neural layers, and shrinks it to a compact 256-number fingerprint normalized on a sphere for ultra-fast search.
$$\text{768-dim Neural Output} \xrightarrow{\text{Dense Layers}} \text{256-dim Compact Vector} \xrightarrow{\text{L2 Normalize}} \text{Final Fingerprint } \tilde{\mathbf{z}}$$
Method 1

SimCSE: Self-Teaching Without Any Human Labels

How It Works (In Plain English)

Imagine showing a student the exact same paragraph twice, but with slightly different random words blurred out each time.

  • Step 1: Feed a complaint into the AI twice.
  • Step 2: Random 10% dropout creates two slightly different views of the exact same story.
  • Step 3: Train the AI: "These two views belong together. Push every other complaint in the room away."
$$\text{Loss} = -\log \frac{\text{Similarity of same story (with noise)}}{\text{Sum of similarities to all other stories}}$$

Results from Run R2

How Fast It Learned: Converged in 1,750 steps (~0.9 epochs)
Did it fix collapse?: Yes, completely uncollapsed the space
Accuracy (Precision@10): 27.24% (Nearly doubled the 14% baseline)

Takeaway: Great at fixing vector collapse, but still didn't know which distinct complaints shared legal meanings.

Method 2 (The Winner)

SupCon: Learning from Real Customer Complaints

How It Works (In Plain English)

Instead of looking at just one sentence with noise, SupCon teaches the AI by comparing real complaints written by different people:

  • Positive Match: Two different consumers both reporting illegal debt collection calls. The AI pulls them together.
  • Negative Match: A consumer reporting a mortgage foreclosure. The AI pushes it away with an enforced margin.
$$\text{Loss} = \max\left(0, \text{Distance(Same Category)} - \text{Distance(Different Domain)} + \text{Margin}\right)$$

Results from Run R4

Training Depth: 9,505 steps (into Epoch 5)
Accuracy (Precision@10): 48.97% (+34.86% over baseline)
Beat Keyword Search?: Yes, beat TF-IDF by +15.43%

✓ Formally promoted as our production retrieval engine.
