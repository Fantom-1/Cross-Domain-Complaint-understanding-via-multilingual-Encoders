The Challenge

Why Out-of-the-Box AI Failed at First

1. The "Vector Collapse" Problem

When raw foundation models like XLM-V read financial complaints without fine-tuning, they suffer from representation collapse.

The AI thought almost every complaint sounded 96% identical to every other complaint, regardless of whether it was about medical debt, an overdraft fee, or a mortgage.

Pretrained AI Accuracy (Precision@10):14.11%
Pretrained AI MRR@10:0.3260
Pretrained AI NDCG@10:0.1497
Basic Keyword Search (TF-IDF):33.54%

The 778M parameter neural model initially lost to basic keyword matching across all metrics.

2. The Contrastive Solution

To fix this, we trained the AI using contrastive metric learning:

Pull Together (Alignment): Complaints describing the exact same consumer problem are pulled tightly together, even if phrased differently.
Push Apart (Uniformity): Complaints about different financial topics are forcibly pushed apart across the entire vector space.

$$\mathbf{h} = \text{MeanPool}(\mathbf{H}_{\text{last}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{768} \xrightarrow{\text{Dense + GELU}} \mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^{256} \xrightarrow{\text{L2 Normalize}} \tilde{\mathbf{z}} \in \mathcal{S}^{255}$$
Method 1

SimCSE: Self-Teaching Without Any Human Labels

How It Works (In Plain English)

Imagine showing a student the exact same paragraph twice, but with slightly different random words blurred out each time.

  • Step 1: Feed a complaint into the AI twice.
  • Step 2: Random 10% dropout creates two slightly different views of the exact same story.
  • Step 3: Train the AI: "These two views belong together. Push every other complaint in the room away."
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{SimCSE}} = -\log \frac{\exp\left(\text{sim}(\mathbf{z}_i, \mathbf{z}_i^+) / \tau\right)}{\sum_{j=1}^B \exp\left(\text{sim}(\mathbf{z}_i, \mathbf{z}_j^+) / \tau\right)}, \quad \tau = 0.05$$

Results & Gains from Run R2

Precision@10: 27.24% +13.13% vs Frozen
MRR@10: 0.4725 +45.0% vs Frozen
NDCG@10: 0.2829 +89.0% vs Frozen
Convergence Speed: 1,750 steps (~0.9 epochs)

Takeaway: Substantially fixed vector collapse, but still lacked fine-grained legal category boundaries.

Method 2 (The Winner)

SupCon: Learning from Real Customer Complaints

How It Works (In Plain English)

Instead of looking at just one sentence with noise, SupCon teaches the AI by comparing real complaints written by different people:

  • Positive Match: Two different consumers both reporting illegal debt collection calls $\rightarrow$ The AI pulls them together.
  • Negative Match: A consumer reporting mortgage foreclosure $\rightarrow$ The AI pushes it away with an enforced margin.
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{SupCon}} = \max\left(0, \|\mathbf{z}_i - \mathbf{z}_p\|_2^2 - \|\mathbf{z}_i - \mathbf{z}_n\|_2^2 + \alpha\right), \quad \alpha = 0.2$$

Results & Massive Gains from Run R4

MRR@10: 0.5619 +72.4% vs Frozen
NDCG@10: 0.4889 +226.6% (3.27x gain)
